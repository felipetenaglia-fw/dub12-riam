Lesson Notes

Context: Piano lesson one-to-one, Professor Terese Fahey with student Rachael O'Hara, Olivier Messiaen, Le baiser de l'Enfant-Jésus.


Professor: This piece is about the infant Jesus, and later the idea of love surrounding us, especially in the F sharp major section, Messiaen's "key of ecstasy".


Professor: Be careful with the accents, sometimes the accents are underneath, in the inner voices, not in the melodic line.


Professor: Let's go from the beginning. Instead of starting too directly, can you fade into the sound?


[Student plays]


Professor: I hear the baby breathing, the intake of breath, then the exhalation. Connect it so it becomes one long phrase, not two separate phrases. Make every note speak.


Professor: When you see diminuendo, it does not mean "suddenly soft". Keep the underlying dynamic, do not come down too soon.


Professor: Messiaen's sound world is far removed from Debussy. You need a slightly pinging, metallic clarity, like a triangle in an orchestra. Your fifth finger needs to curve more, like a pizzicato on a violin.


Professor: Going into the big crescendo, hold back. Promise the dynamic, but do not give it yet, that creates tension, then we erupt.


Professor: Then we arrive at the moment of ecstasy, F sharp major, it says avec amour, "with love". Do not sanitise what comes before, I want more drive into it.


[Student plays]


Professor: Much better. Those arpeggios should be like little pinpricks of light, like fireworks. There has to be life in each note.
